## About

I've spent more than 20 years working where software, organisations and change meet. I started as a developer, then moved into architecture, technical leadership, consulting and management, and eventually back to hands-on engineering. What ties it together is an interest in systems: technical and organisational systems, how work flows through them, and why their performance degrades over time.

Today I'm a Senior Software Engineer in the Australian energy market, building and maintaining distributed data services with C#, SQL Server, Docker and Terraform on Microsoft Azure.

### Engineering

I've written software for most of my career: DB2 and early Java at Bankwest, enterprise Java architecture for BHP mine sites, and the payment engine at Tyro, built with test-driven development and pair programming. Today it's C#/.NET data services on Azure. I stayed close to the code through my consulting and management years too, and I care about systems that stay reliable and understandable long after the first release.

### Leadership

I began by leading small teams at Bankwest, Ajilon and SSFS. Later I opened and ran the ThoughtWorks Perth office, and led Professional Services at Lexicon as it grew from 78 to 137 people. I've done the full range of the work, from hiring and structure design to coaching and the hard performance conversations. My focus is on setting up the conditions for good work.

### Consulting & Transformation

For more than a decade at ThoughtWorks and Reconceive, I partnered with executives at NAB, ANZ, Telstra, Suncorp, Kmart, Insignia (IOOF) and the ABS. I used systems thinking, the Vanguard Method and Systems Leadership to improve how their delivery systems performed. Results included cutting withdrawal times from 28 days to 4, making firewall-change turnaround 70% faster, and reducing failure demand from 96% to 20%.

### What I'm looking for

I'm interested in Engineering Manager and Principal-level roles that combine technical depth with leadership. I don't see engineering management as stepping away from engineering. I see it as shaping the conditions in which good engineering becomes possible.
